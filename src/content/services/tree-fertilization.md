## Which Fertilization Approach Do You Actually Need?

Three situations get called "fertilization" and are not the same job. **A young or newly
planted tree** often needs a light, establishment-focused feeding to help roots take
hold, a smaller job than a mature tree needs. **A mature tree showing thin canopy or
slow growth** usually calls for deep-root or root-zone feeding matched to a soil test,
since a surface spread rarely reaches roots that run several feet down. **A tree with a
diagnosed disease entity**, verticillium wilt or a canker, needs the disease addressed
first, and fertilization plays a supporting role rather than the whole fix. The
assessment is what tells you which of the three you actually have.

## What Goes Wrong With Tree Fertilization Done Badly in Hickory

A surface-spread fertilizer with no soil test behind it can miss the actual deficiency
entirely, feeding a tree more of what it already has enough of while the real shortage
goes uncorrected. Over-fertilizing pushes fast, weak growth that is more vulnerable to
storm damage rather than a stronger tree. Feeding a tree with an active disease entity
instead of addressing the disease itself can mask decline for a season while the
underlying problem keeps spreading. Skipping the soil check on Hickory's sandy loam in
particular risks applying a feeding that leaches past the root zone before the tree
gets the benefit.

## What Tree Fertilization Includes in Hickory (and What It Doesn't)

We diagnose nutrient deficiencies, test soil at the root zone, and apply root-zone or
deep-root fertilization matched to species and soil condition. We do not apply
restricted-use pesticide, fungicide or herbicide for active disease treatment. That
specific work calls for a state pesticide applicator license, so we refer it to a
licensed applicator when a diagnosis turns up a disease entity instead of a nutrient
gap. Basic soil amendment and fertilization do not require that license, and it is the
service this page covers.
