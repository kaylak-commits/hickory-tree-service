## Why Storm Damage Hits Trees Hard in Hickory, NC

Hickory gets about 45 inches of rain a year, and the sandy loam soil under most yards
saturates during a heavy storm rather than draining it off. Saturated ground holds a
tree's roots far less firmly, which is why a storm here is more likely to send a
whole tree over than just break a few branches. Loblolly pine, one of the most common
trees in this area, is shallow-rooted for its height and is often the first to go
over in high wind and wet ground. Water oak and red maple fail differently: instead
of uprooting whole, they tend to split at a codominant union, two competing stems
that tear apart under wind or ice load rather than one solid trunk. A tree that
survives one storm with hidden root or trunk damage is often the one that comes down
in the next.

## What Kind of Storm Damage Are You Dealing With?

Four situations get the call after a storm, and each one changes what happens first.
**A tree resting on a house or garage** needs the weight assessed before anything is
cut, since removing the wrong piece first can let the tree shift further into the
structure. **A tree or limb blocking a driveway or the road** usually gets cleared
first for access, even if a full cleanup comes later. **A hung limb or leaning tree
still attached overhead** is often the most dangerous of the four, since it can drop
with no warning, and it gets roped and lowered under control rather than cut free.
**A tree already down in the yard**, clear of any structure, is usually the most
straightforward, cut and hauled with no special rigging required. If a tree has come
down on a power line, do not approach it, and call the utility company before anyone
else.

## How Long Emergency Tree Removal Takes

A tree already down and clear of a structure can be cleared in a few hours. A tree
resting on a roof or a vehicle takes longer. The weight has to be assessed first,
then often lifted with a crane rather than cut away piece by piece, since cutting
risks the tree shifting further into whatever it is resting on. A hung limb still
attached overhead also takes careful time, since it gets lowered under control on a
rope rather than dropped. Multiple trees down after the same storm add time in the
order the calls come in and the danger each one presents.

## How an Emergency Removal Runs, Start to Finish

Every emergency call follows the same four stages, whether it is one limb or a tree
through a roof.

### Phone Assessment
We ask what the tree is resting on, whether it is still attached or fully down, and
whether it has come down on a power line. That determines what equipment goes out and
in what order calls get handled.

### Checking for Hazards Before Cutting
The crew checks for live power lines, unstable footing and whether the tree is still
shifting before any cut is made. A tree resting on a structure gets its weight and
lean assessed before a piece comes off.

### Getting the Tree Down Safely
A tree on a structure is lifted or cut in sections to avoid further damage. A hung
limb is roped and lowered rather than cut free and dropped. A tree already on the
ground is cut and hauled with standard equipment.

### Cleanup and Debris Haul
Limbs, trunk sections and debris get hauled off, and the site is left clear. If you
want the stump ground the same visit, that is available too.

## What Goes Wrong When Storm Cleanup Is Done Badly

Cutting into a tree still under tension from a roofline, a fence, or another tree can
cause it to spring or roll unpredictably.  Removing the wrong piece first from a tree
resting on a structure can let the remaining weight shift and cause more damage than
the original fall did. A hung limb cut free without being roped first drops with no
warning and no control over where it lands. Skipping the phone assessment is the most
common shortcut. It sends a crew in blind, without knowing whether power lines are
involved, which makes it the most dangerous one too.
