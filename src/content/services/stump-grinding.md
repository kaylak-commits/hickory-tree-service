## Which Stump Grinding Job Do You Actually Need?

Three situations get called "stump grinding" and they price differently. **A single
recent stump** from a removal we just did grinds fastest, since there is no old root
flare to work around. **An old stump left by another crew**, often partly grown over
or with grass established around it, takes longer to locate the true edge of and grind
clean. **Multiple stumps on one property**, from a lot that has had several trees come
down over the years, price as a group visit rather than separate callouts. If you plan
to replant in the same spot, tell us before the grinder starts, since grinding depth
changes when new roots need room to establish.

## How Long Stump Grinding Takes

A single small stump usually grinds out in under an hour once the crew is set up. A
wide or old stump with an established root flare takes longer, since the grinder works
in from the edges rather than through the center in one pass. Multiple stumps on one
property add time per stump rather than a flat rate for the visit. You get a time
estimate when we look at the actual stump, not a generic range.

## How a Stump Grinding Job Runs, Start to Finish

Every stump grinding job follows the same four stages, whether it is one stump or a
whole lot.

### Inspection and a Written Price
We look at the stump's diameter, how deep the root flare sits, and what is nearby, a
fence, a walkway, a septic line, that the grinder needs to avoid. You get a price
before the machine starts.

### Protecting Your Property
The grinder throws chips and fine debris while it works, so anything close by, a
flower bed, a fence line, gets covered or moved first.

### The Grinding Itself
The stump gets ground down below grade, typically 4 to 6 inches, so the area can be
covered with soil and grass or replanted without a stump visible or in the way of a
mower.

### Cleanup and Chip Removal
Leftover stump chips get raked out and hauled off, or left in a pile for you to keep for mulch, at your choice. The area is left level and walkable.

## What Goes Wrong When Stump Grinding Is Done Badly

Grinding shallow instead of down to a proper depth leaves a stump that resurfaces as
the ground settles, or that a mower catches a year later. Skipping a check for nearby
utility lines or a septic line before grinding can turn a routine job into an expensive
repair. Leaving the root flare instead of a large main root uncut lets suckers keep
sending up new growth around the old stump for years, particularly with red maple.
Grinding without hauling or spreading the chips leaves a pile that has to be dealt with
separately, an extra trip that should have been part of the same visit.

## What We Grind To

Stumps get ground to 4 to 6 inches below grade as standard, deep enough to cover with
soil and reseed or lay sod without a bump showing. A job going in ahead of new
construction or a driveway gets ground deeper on request, since equipment weight and
grading tolerances are different from a lawn. Large surface roots radiating out from
the stump get ground back several feet where they would otherwise interfere with
mowing or a walkway.

## What We Do and What We Don't

We grind stumps of any size, single or multiple, fresh or old. We do not fill or
regrade the resulting hole beyond raking the chips level, that is landscaping work we
can point you toward. We do not remove the stump's root system entirely, that would
mean excavating the yard rather than grinding, and is rarely worth the cost for a
residential lot. If a stump sits directly over a septic line or a utility line, we say
so at the inspection rather than grinding blind.
