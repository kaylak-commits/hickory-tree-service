## Why Trees Come Down More Often in Hickory

Hickory gets about 45 inches of rain a year over roughly 107 days, well above the national
average, and the ground underneath most yards here is sandy loam. Sandy loam drains
faster than clay, but it still saturates during a wet stretch, and it holds a tree's roots
less firmly than denser soil to begin with. That combination is why mature trees in this
area are more likely to uproot whole in a storm than to simply drop a few limbs.

Winter adds a second reason trees fail here. Catawba County sees close to 85 freezing
days a year, and each freeze-thaw cycle widens whatever crack or weak union was already
sitting in a trunk or at the base of the roots. A tree that looked stable in October can
fail in February from damage the cold did over several months, not from the storm that
finally brought it down. Loblolly pine is shallow-rooted for its height and is one of the
first species to go over in saturated ground. Water oak and red maple carry a different
risk, splitting at a codominant trunk (a double leader with a weak union) rather than uprooting outright. The
inspection identifies which of these a given tree is heading toward before the crew
ever picks up a saw.

## Which Tree Service Do You Actually Need?

Four situations get called "tree removal" and they price and schedule differently. A
**dead or dying tree still standing** usually needs full removal once enough canopy is
gone that the tree cannot recover, rather than [trimming](/tree-trimming), which only
helps a tree that still has healthy growth to work with. A **leaning tree after a
storm** may only need cabling or bracing, and only if the root plate is still mostly
intact. A tree that has visibly lifted the soil on one side is coming down regardless.
A **tree with a split trunk or a double leader that has opened up at the union** is a
removal candidate regardless of canopy health, because that weak point will fail again.
A **stump left from an old removal** is a grinding job only, no crew or crane required.
If you are not sure which describes your tree, the inspection is free, including if the
honest answer is that it does not need to come down.

## How Long a Tree Removal Takes in Hickory

A tree removal has no flat number for duration. Three things drive it: whether the tree
drops whole or comes down in sections, whether the crew climbs or works from the ground,
and how close the tree sits to a structure. A tree in an open yard with a clear drop zone
can be down within a couple of hours. A tree that has to be climbed and rigged down in
pieces next to a structure takes considerably longer, since every section gets lowered
on a rope rather than dropped. You get a time estimate at the inspection, based on your
actual tree and yard, not a generic range.

## How a Tree Removal Runs in Hickory, Start to Finish

Every removal follows the same four stages, whether the tree is a small dead pine or a
large oak next to the house.

### Inspection and a Written Price
We look at the tree's lean, its root condition, its distance from structures and power
lines, and whether it can be dropped whole or needs to come down in sections. You get a
price before anything is cut.

### Protecting Your Property
Drop zones get marked off around the tree, and anything within reach of falling limbs,
fencing, garden beds, a driveway, gets covered or moved. On rigged jobs, ropes and
rigging points get set before the first cut.

### The Removal Itself
Open-yard trees are felled in one piece. Trees near a structure come down in sections,
each piece lowered by rope rather than dropped, working from the top of the canopy down to
the trunk.

### Cleanup and Stump Options
Limbs and trunk sections get hauled off the same day. If you want the stump ground, that
happens in the same visit rather than a second trip.

## What Goes Wrong When Tree Removal Is Done Badly in Hickory

A section of tree dropped without rigging can swing into a fence, a roof or a power line
on the way down. A trunk felled without reading the lean correctly can land in the
opposite direction the crew expected, damaging the exact structure the job was hired to
protect. Roots left in the ground after a rushed job send up suckers for years. Red maple
roots specifically can go on to lift a walkway or invade a septic line long after the
tree is gone. Skipping the inspection step is the most common shortcut, and the one that
turns a routine removal into a homeowner's insurance claim over damage that did not
have to happen.

## Tree Removal Regulations in Hickory

Removing a tree on your own residential property in Hickory does not require a city
permit. The City of Hickory's Land Development Code exempts single-family and two-family
residential lots from its tree provisions entirely. A permit only applies to a tree
growing in a street right-of-way or on public property, or to a removal tied to new
commercial or multi-family construction. If your tree sits in the right-of-way or the job
is tied to new construction, we handle that permit for you. Water oak can be hollow
inside while looking healthy from the outside, so a crew checks each one for rot and
weak spots before setting a felling plan, not just cutting on sight.
