## Which Arborist Service Do You Actually Need?

Three calls come in sounding the same and are not. **A tree that looks stressed but is
still leafing out** usually needs a health assessment first, not a removal quote. The
cause could be soil, a root problem or an early-stage disease that responds to treatment. **A tree an assessment already flagged as nutrient-deficient** moves to
fertilization and soil work rather than another inspection. **A tree with a confirmed
structural failure**, a split union, a dead crown, a root plate that has let go, is a
removal call. An honest arborist says so instead of prescribing treatment on a tree past
saving. The assessment is what sorts these three apart before money gets spent
on the wrong one.

## How an Arborist Assessment Runs, Start to Finish

### Site Walk and History
The arborist walks the property, asks what changed recently, storm damage, construction,
new grading, and looks at species, canopy condition and soil at the base of each tree in
question.

### Diagnosis Against ANSI A300
Findings get checked against ANSI A300 guidance on structure, pruning history and risk,
rather than a guess based on how the tree looks from the driveway.

### The Recommendation
The assessment ends with a plain recommendation for the tree: treat, monitor, prune,
cable, or remove, with the reasoning behind it, not just the verdict.

### The Written Record
The arborist writes up the tree's condition the same visit or shortly after, a report
useful for your own records, an insurer or a buyer's inspection.

## What Goes Wrong Without a Real Arborist Assessment

A removal crew without arborist training can misread a tree that is actually treatable and
recommend cutting it down, costing you a healthy, mature tree that soil work or pruning
could have saved. The reverse also happens on a job like this. A tree with a hidden structural defect, a
split union under bark that has not yet failed, gets left standing on a visual check
alone. It then comes down in the next storm instead of on a planned schedule. A treatment
recommendation made without checking the actual disease or pest present can also waste
money on the wrong fix, since a soil deficiency and a fungal pathogen do not respond to
the same correction.

## Standards We Work To

ANSI A300 sets the national standard for tree pruning, cabling, fertilization and risk
assessment, and every arborist visit here is worked against it rather than a general
sense of what looks right. That means pruning cuts sized correctly to the branch collar,
a documented basis for any removal recommendation, and a soil or root diagnosis before a
fertilization plan gets written, not after.

## What Arborist Services Include (and What They Don't)

We diagnose tree health, assess structural risk, and recommend the right next step,
treatment, pruning, cabling or removal. We do not apply restricted-use pesticide or
fungicide for disease treatment ourselves, that work calls for a state pesticide
applicator license, and we refer it to a licensed applicator when a diagnosis calls for
it. We do not perform legal tree-value appraisals for court, though our written report
can support one done by the right specialist.

## What a Typical Arborist Assessment Looks Like

A homeowner calls about a red maple with leaves browning on one side of the canopy while
the rest of the tree looks healthy. The arborist checks the trunk, root flare and soil
around the base, and finds early signs consistent with verticillium wilt on that side
rather than storm or root damage. The recommendation is monitoring plus a soil-focused
fertilization plan rather than removal, since the tree is not yet a structural risk. A
written summary goes to the homeowner the same day, with a follow-up assessment
suggested for next season.
