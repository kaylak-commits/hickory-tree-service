## What Makes a Tree Hazardous?

Four signs mean a tree needs a real look instead of just watching it. **A visible
lean that is new or getting worse**, especially with lifted soil on one side of the
root plate, means the roots are losing their grip. **Dead limbs or a thinning
canopy** on part of the tree while the rest looks healthy often signals disease or root
damage on that side. **A split trunk or a codominant union with a visible crack** is a
structural failure waiting on the next load, wind or ice. **A tree standing close to a
structure, driveway or power line** raises the stakes of any of the above, since a
failure there does real damage rather than falling into open yard. If you see any of
these, the assessment is free, including if the honest answer is that the tree can be
monitored rather than removed.

## How a Hazardous Tree Removal Runs, Start to Finish

Every hazardous removal follows the same four stages, whether the risk is a lean, a
split trunk or a dead canopy.

### Assessment and a Written Price
We look at the lean, the root condition, any visible splits, and how close the tree
stands to a structure, fence or power line. You get a price before anything is cut.

### Planning the Drop or the Rig
A tree that can be dropped safely into open space gets planned that way. A tree too
close to anything worth protecting gets rigged for a sectional, controlled removal
instead.

### The Removal Itself
A tree cleared to drop safely comes down whole. One assessed as too close to a
structure gets taken apart from the top, each section lowered by rope or crane so
nothing swings free on the way down.

### Cleanup and Stump Options
Limbs and trunk sections get hauled off the same day. If you want the stump ground,
that is available in the same visit.

## What Goes Wrong When a Hazardous Tree Is Left Alone or Rushed

A tree with a worsening lean that gets ignored eventually fails, often in the next storm rather than on a predictable schedule. By then it may be too close to a structure to remove without risk to what is nearby. Felling a leaning tree in the
wrong direction, misreading how much the root plate has already let go, can send it
into the exact structure the removal was meant to protect. Skipping the assessment
step and cutting into a tree that turns out to be hollow or already split can cause it
to fail unpredictably mid-cut. A dead or diseased tree left standing near a property
line can also spread the same disease or pest pressure to healthy trees nearby.
