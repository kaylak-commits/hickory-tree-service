## Do You Need a Haul-Off or Just Chipping?

Two outcomes get requested under the same word, and they leave a yard looking very different afterward. **Full haul-off** removes every limb, trunk section and pile of brush from the property, leaving a clear yard with nothing left behind. **Chipping in place** runs the smaller material through a chipper and leaves the resulting mulch on site. That is useful if you want ground cover for a bed or a bare patch instead of paying to haul it away. Larger trunk sections do not chip well and usually get hauled regardless of which option you choose for the smaller material. Some homeowners also ask us to leave cut rounds for firewood rather than hauling or chipping them, which we are glad to do when the wood is a usable size and species.

## How a Debris Hauling Job Runs, Start to Finish

Every standalone hauling job runs through the same four stages, separate from a job that already includes it.

### Walk-Through and a Written Price
We look at the volume and type of material, whether it needs sectioning first, and whether you want a full haul-off or chipping left in place, then give a written price.

### Sorting and Sectioning
Any trunk section too large to lift or load whole gets cut down further, and material gets sorted into what chips easily and what needs to be hauled whole.

### Chipping or Loading
Brush and smaller limbs go through the chipper if you have chosen that option, and everything else gets loaded onto the truck for haul-off.

### Final Cleanup
The yard gets a final pass to catch smaller debris the equipment missed, leaving the property clear and walkable before the crew leaves.

## What Goes Wrong When Debris Is Left On Site

A pile of limbs and brush left against a fence or structure becomes a fire hazard that sits there long after the original job is finished. Wet debris left on a lawn for weeks can kill the grass underneath and leave a bare patch that takes a season to recover. Storm-downed material left in a yard can hide a hazard, a broken branch under leaves, or attract pests looking for shelter in a wet pile. Trunk sections too heavy to move by hand, left behind by a crew without the right equipment, often turn into a second, more expensive call once a homeowner tries to deal with them alone.
